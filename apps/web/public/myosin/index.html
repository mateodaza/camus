The problem

Creating is cheap.
Verifying is not.

Anyone can put a client-ready paragraph on the page in seconds. The same tool writes a confidently wrong one just as fast, and on the page you cannot tell them apart.

The old way of using AI was to prompt it and read what came back. Verifying, testing and fact-checking that text is where looping comes in.

What looping means

It doesn’t stop at the first draft.

Prompting. You ask, it answers, and checking the answer is your problem.

Looping. One model writes, another goes at it looking for what is wrong, the first repairs it, and round it goes again.

Draft→Challenge Repair

It stops when the checks come back clean, or when it hits a decision only you can make. It cannot go round forever.

Who does what

The jobs stay separate.

Maker

Writes the work, and repairs it when the review finds something.

Auditor

A model from another company, told to find what is wrong.

Checks

Mechanical rules. A citation resolves or it does not. Nothing to argue with.

You

Real disagreements come to you rather than being settled by a model.

Two models from different companies are less likely to be wrong in the same way. That does not guarantee correctness, and Camus never claims it does.

Your own knowledge

Your best strategist is already in the room.

Hivemind already holds the knowledge this team has captured: the projects, the research, the positioning calls. A model has none of that. Point the loop at Hivemind and it pulls the relevant pieces, freezes them, and argues from them.

The sealed run record names which pieces it used, so a claim traces back to something Myosin actually knows. And the better those connections get, the more of it the loop can use.

What you point it at

Four kinds of job.

Research memo

An answer you can defend, every number traced to a source.

“Should we lead Q4 with founder content or client case studies?”

Competitor teardown

Positioning, channels, and what we take from it.

“How is that competitor actually winning on LinkedIn?”

Freeform

Anything written. Ground it in Hivemind or not, as the job needs.

“Turn these notes into a launch announcement.”

Build: code

A repository instead of a document. Independent review plus your own tests.

“Point it at the repo and harden this function.”

How you use it

Three decisions, then run.

  1. Describe the job Brief it the way you would brief a strategist.
  2. Set the standard What has to be true for you to trust the result.
  3. Choose the models Who writes, who checks. Then press Run.

From there it plans and runs the loop, stopping to ask you when the answer is genuinely yours to give. Watch it work in the browser, or start it from the command line and come back to the same sealed run record.

It runs on your machine, signed in with your own model accounts. The work is coordinated on your machine and the records stay there. Drafts go to the same providers you already use, through your own login, so nothing routes through us.

The receipt

Don’t take its word for it.

A receipt is the run’s record, sealed when it finishes and readable afterwards by anyone. It records what happened: the models that actually ran, the evidence retrieved, each finding, each decision you made, and the standard the work was held to.

Fair questions

The ones you’d ask anyway.

Why not just one LLM? A model checking its own work can carry the writer’s blind spots. A second company is less likely to miss the same things.

What does Hivemind bring? The knowledge the team has captured in Hivemind. The model brings horsepower, Hivemind brings Myosin’s judgement, and the draft argues from that rather than the open internet.

How do you pick the two models? The writer for fit with the job, the checker for independence. Swapping either is a setting, and the receipt shows who really ran. As better models ship, you point it at those.

Should I ask for a harder check? In two comparisons, higher requested effort used up to 2.7× the output tokens without changing either verdict. Start low, then escalate where a mistake would be costly.

Where does this go next? Better connections into Hivemind are being built. As those land, the loop reaches more of what Myosin already has, and the same checking applies to all of it.

Five places to point it.

  1. Research “Should we lead Q4 with founder content or client case studies?”
  2. Teardown “How is that competitor actually winning on LinkedIn?”
  3. Launch “Turn these messy notes into an announcement we can defend.”
  4. Case study “Pull what Hivemind knows about this project into a cited draft.”
  5. Reaction “Draft our take on this week’s platform change, every claim sourced.”

These are ideas for a first pilot rather than promises. Today you’ll watch one job run end to end, and everything else comes from a real run that replays from its sealed receipt.
